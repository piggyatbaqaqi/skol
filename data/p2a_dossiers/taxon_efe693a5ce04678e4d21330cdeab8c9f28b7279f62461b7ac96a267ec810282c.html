However, the BLASTn results for the ITS sequence of Digitodochium anshunense did not retrieve any close hits, showing 90% similarity to the same reference strain. Morphologically, both species are similar in having ascospores with a basal dwarf cell and lack a germ slit. However, A. eucalyptorum can be differentiated from D. anshunense by its occurrence on the leaves of Eucalyptus spp. (vs. bamboo for D. anshunense), smaller ascomata up to 200 µm diam. (vs. 570–825 µm wide), smaller asci 60– 85 × 5–6 µm (vs. 120–185 × 6.5–9 µm), and smaller ascospores with a larger brown cell (7–10 × 4–5.5 µm) and lack a mucilaginous sheath (vs. larger brown cells 15–17.6 × 5.5–8 µm, surrounded by a thin, inconspicuous mucilaginous sheath) (Crous et al. 2006). Morphologically, Digitodochium anshunense is also similar to Anthostomella clypeoides in having ascospores with a basal dwarf cell, lacking a germ slit and surrounded by a thin mucilaginous sheath. However, A. clypeoides differs from D. anshunense in being reported on Rubus and Epilobium spp., having smaller asci 65–81 × 6–8 µm, and smaller (10–14.5 µm) inequilateral ellipsoid ascospores with one side flattened (Francis 1975, Lu and Hyde 2000). Based on the key comparisons provided by Lu and Hyde (2000), Digitodochium anshunense is morphologically similar to Anthostomella appendiculosa, A. triangularis, and A. colligata, but it can be differentiated from these species by its host, as well as the size and morphology of its ascospores and asci. Anthostomella appendiculosa has been reported on Rubus spp. and possesses larger ascospores (28–36 × 8–10 µm) with a germ slit and a cordate-shaped dwarf cell. Anthostomella triangularis, reported on Pinus spp., produces limoniform to triangular smaller ascospores 9.5–11.5 × 5–5.5 µm. Anthostomella colligata, found on Raphia spp. (Palmae), has larger ascospores (17.5–26 × 9–11.5 µm) that are surrounded by a thicker mucilaginous sheath (Lu and Hyde 2000). Species of Occultitheca also possess two-celled ascospores of similar size to that of Digitodochium anshunense. Occultitheca species are differentiated from anthostomella-like species by a larger distance between the uppermost ascospore and the ascus apex (Rogers and Ju 2003). We believe this is not a significant distinguishing character for differentiating the two genera, as species such as Anthostomella formosa, A. clypeoides, and A. saltensis exhibit a similar distance between the uppermost ascospore and the ascus apex as seen in Occultitheca species (Daranagama et al. 2015, Lu and Hyde 2000). Occultitheca rosae has similar ascospore dimensions (16.5–20 × 6.5–8 µm), covered with a thin mucilaginous sheath. However, it differs from D. anshunense by being reported on Rosa spp., having smaller ascomata 360–385 × 350–420 µm (vs. 570–825 × 450–675 µm), smaller asci 90– 140 × 11–13 µm, and ascospores with a straight germ slit extending along the entire spore length (Samarakoon et al. 2022). Occultitheca costaricensis can be distinguished from D. anshunense by its aggregated ascomata (2–12 in a cluster), ellipsoid-inequilateral ascospores with a straight germ slit, and a larger basal hyaline cell (1.5–4.5 × 1.5–4 µm) (Rogers and Ju 2003). In comparison to Digitodochium species, D. anshunense closely resembles D. damingshanense and D. xishuangbannense.     Magnostiolata Samarak. & K.D. Hyde, Fungal Diversity 112: 65 (2022) Notes – Magnostiolata was introduced by Samarakoon et al. (2022) to accommodate Anthostomella-like taxa distinguished by funnel-shaped ostiolar canals filled with amorphous cells and ascospores enveloped in a wing-shaped mucilaginous sheath. Later, Liu et al. (2025) expanded the genus by proposing a new combination and describing a new species. In this study, we introduce another new species of Magnostiolata, which phylogenetically clusters within the Magnostiolata clade with strong support.
