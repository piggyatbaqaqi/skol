    2. Materials and Methods2.1. Isolation and CultureOne protosteloid strain, YIP-40, was isolated from the standing
dead leaves of Rumex acetosa L. collected from
Sugadaira Highland (Nagano, Japan, 1300 m altitude). Substrates were
soaked in DW for 40 min and inoculated to wMY agar (0.02 g malt
extract, 0.02 g yeast extract, 0.75 g K2HPO4, 15
g agar, 1 L DW, sensu Spiegel,
1990) as a primary isolation plate (PIP). The PIP was observed
with a light microscope LABOPHOT2 (Nikon, Tokyo, Japan) equipped with
long working distance lenses. From the emerged protosteloid sporocarps
on the PIP, single spores were picked up by using a thin tungsten
needle. The spores were inoculated onto another wMY agar plate fed
with Escherichia coli (Migula) Castellani ＆ Chalmers
and unidentified bacterium isolated from PIP, and incubated at 20 °C.

The spores were globose, (7.7-)9.8(-13.6) µm in diam, typically uninucleate, and ornamented by fine spines, approximately 100 nm in height (Figs. 1A-E, 2A-C). The stalks were straight, (25.4-) 51.8(-95.3) µm in length, hollow at the base and continued to the basal disc (Fig. 1F). The hilum, a disc-like structure projected very slightly and was situated on the spore at the junction with the stalk (Figs. 1C, 2A-C). The prespore cells were ellipsoid (right cell in Fig. 1G) and became globose with development (left cell in Fig. 1G).
