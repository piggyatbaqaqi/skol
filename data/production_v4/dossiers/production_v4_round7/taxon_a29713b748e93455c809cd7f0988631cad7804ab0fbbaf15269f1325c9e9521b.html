    Sarcosomataceae Kobayasi, J. Jap. Bot.: 516 (1937)
  • diagnosis
    Plectania was reported as a saprobic genus with P. melastoma as the type species (Korf 1972,
Zeng et al. 2021). Plectania is distinguished by its discoid to cupulate, substipitate to stipitate
apothecia, ellipsoid to kidney-shaped ascospores with a smooth or transversally furrowed wall
(Zhuang et al. 2004, Carbone et al. 2013, Ekanayaka et al. 2018, Zeng et al. 2021). Plectania is a
polyphyletic genus and forms a clade with Galiella in the combined ITS and LSU phylogenetic
studies (Zeng et al. 2021). However, Plectania differs from Galiella in having larger, lighter
colored apothecia, and a much more extensive, gelatinous medullary excipulum (Korf 1957).
  • description
    Saprobic on dead pine needles. Sexual morph: Apothecia 0.3–0.5 cm high, 0.3–0.8 cm diam.
when fresh, superficial, scattered, urceolate to cupulate, sessile, leathery. Disc dark rufous, slightly
rough. Receptacle dark, slightly rough. Margin obviously involute, slightly fragile, gray. Ectal
excipulum 60‒150 µm (x̅  = 95 µm, n = 5) thick, with two layers, brown to dark brown outer layer
with carbonaceous, thick-walled cells of textura globulosa, 4.6‒13.6 × 3.9−9.5 µm (x̅  = 8.9 × 6.7
µm, n = 42), light brown to hyaline inner layer with thin-walled cells of textura angularis, 7.8‒24.2
× 5.8‒13.6 µm (x̅  = 15.4 × 9 µm, n = 66). Medullary excipulum 180‒255 µm (x̅  = 187 µm, n = 5)
thick, hyaline, with thin-walled cells of textura intricate, 2.4‒5 µm (x̅  = 3.6 µm, n = 67) diam.
Hymenium 210‒320 µm (x̅  = 269 µm, n = 5) thick, hyaline. Hairs 4.4‒7 µm (x̅  = 5.5 µm, n = 45)
diam., cylindrical, thick-walled, septate, brown. Paraphyses 1.7‒2.6 µm (x̅  = 2.2 µm, n = 20)
diam., filiform, apically and middle branched, slightly rough, thin-walled, septate, hyaline. Asci
370‒440 × 10.8‒17.7 µm (x̅  = 424 × 13.3 µm, n = 25), 8-spored, unitunicate, suboperculate,
cylindrical, hyaline, with the narrow bases and truncated to obtuse apex, inamyloid. Ascospores
16‒23.5 × 8.4‒11 µm (x̅  = 20.7 × 10 µm, n = 52), uniseriate, ellipsoid, hyaline, slightly rough, thinwalled, multi-guttulate. Asexual morph: Undetermined.
  • notes
    Conlarium was introduced by Liu et al. (2012b) with C. dupliciascosporum (sexual morph) as
the type species, which belongs to Conlariaceae (Conlariales, Sordariomycetes). Conlarium are
mainly known by the asexual morph, which is characterized by muriform, irregularly globose or
subglobose conidia (Liu et al. 2012b, Xie et al. 2019, Hyde et al. 2020b, Zhang et al. 2021a).
  • etymology
    Etymology – Referring to the similarity with Plectania milleri

  • description
    Asexual morph: Hyphomycetous. Colonies on natural substrate superficial, sporodochial,
punctiform, gregarious, dark brown to black. Mycelium mostly immersed, comprises branched, pale
brown to hyaline, smooth and thin-walled hyphae. Conidiophores absent or reduced to
conidiogenous cells. Conidiogenous cells monoblastic, holoblastic, integrated, determinate,
doliiform, cylindrical, hyaline to pale brown, smooth. Conidia (28–)36–54(–69) × (23–)31–44(–54)
μm (x̅  =  45 × 38 μm, n  =  35), acrogenous, solitary, dry, muriform, irregular, subglobose to
ellipsoidal, pale to dark brown, 3–6-transversely septate, 3–5-longitudinally septate, slightly
constricted at the septa, smooth, thin-walled.
  • notes
    C. sichuanense (CGMCC 3.20406) are mainly in the 45bp at the end of the LSU gene of
C. sichuanense. This may be a sequence that exceeds the effective read length, and the credibility
of this part of the sequence is low. Morphologically, our new collection fits the generic concept of
Conlarium, with muriform, subglobose to ellipsoidal, irregular, pale to dark brown conidia (Liu et
al. 2012b, Xie et al. 2019, Hyde et al. 2020b, Zhang et al. 2021a). However, our new collection has
larger conidia ((28–)36–54(–69) × (23–)31–44(–54) μm vs. 15.5–19.5 × 13.5–17 μm), more
transverse septate (3–6 vs. 0–2), and longitudinal septate (3–5 vs. 1–3). If the sequence of
C. sichuanense is correct, then our new collection may indeed be a novel species, as they have
significant differences in conidia size. However, considering factors such as habitat and host
influence, it is temporarily identified as C. sichuanense, which was first found in freshwater
habitats.
