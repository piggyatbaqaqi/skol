# `latin_wordlist.txt` — source, licence, and measurements

## What it is

899 973 Latin word forms, one per line, lowercased and folded to
ASCII.  Generated by
[`bin/whitakers_wordlist.py`](../bin/whitakers_wordlist.py) from
Whitaker's *WORDS*.

These are **inflected forms**, not lemmas.  *WORDS* stores stems plus
inflection codes, and the generator joins each stem to every ending
its declension/conjugation licenses — so `albus` expands to 36 forms
including `albae`, `albis`, `albior`, `albissima`.  That is what the
corpus needs; a lemma list would only ever match `albus`.

## Why it exists

The §9 mode-B OCR detector (`D8` in
[`docs/data_quality_production_v4_model.md`](../docs/data_quality_production_v4_model.md))
finds OCR space-displacement by testing whether a run of
out-of-vocabulary tokens rejoins into a real word.  It is blind to
any language it lacks vocabulary for, and Latin diagnoses run
**79.5 %** out-of-vocabulary against an English dictionary: identical
simulated corruption scored 18.18 % on an English passage and 1.33 %
on a Latin one, below the 2 % detection floor.

## The `AGE=G,H` restriction does not work — and why

*WORDS* carries an `AGE` column, and `G` ("Latin post 15th —
Scholarly/Scientific, 16–18") plus `H` ("Coined recently, words for
new things, 19–20") look like exactly the scientific-Latin subset
this corpus wants.  They are not.  Restricting to them gives **3.7 %**
Latin coverage — *worse than FreeDict's 6.1 %* — and barely clears
the detection floor at 2.67 %.

Sampling the 2 101 `G`/`H` entries shows why:

    G  independens   independent;        H  telephonist   telephone operator;
    G  dali          dahlia;             H  telecopiatr   fax machine, fax;
    G  boton         button;             G  alcoholic     alcoholic;
    G  impili        sock;               G  technologi    technology;

It is **Latin for modern concepts** — telephones, fax machines,
alcohol, buttons, socks — the vocabulary of rendering contemporary
life into Latin.  Botanical descriptive Latin is a different thing:
mostly *classical* adjectives used descriptively (`albus`, `latus`,
`carnosus`, `glabra`), plus Greek-rooted neo-Latin coinages
(`basidium`, `ascus`, `pileus`, `acanthocystides`) that *WORDS* does
not carry at any age.  Neither `basidium` nor `sporae` is in the full
list.

The subset can still be regenerated for inspection:

```sh
bin/whitakers_wordlist --words-dir /path/to/whitakers-words \
    --age G,H --output /tmp/latin_scientific.txt
```

It is not checked in: at 328 KB it measures worse than every other
option except having no Latin vocabulary at all.

## Recommendation

**The corpus-derived vocabulary remains the better primary source**:
4 269 forms, 40 KB, and it beats this 899 973-form list on every
detection number (62.2 % vs 58.5 % coverage; 22.67 % vs 14.67 %;
22.19 % vs 16.22 %).  The Latin this corpus uses is descriptive
neo-Latin, and only the corpus has it.

Two reasons to keep this file anyway:

* **Independent failure modes.**  The corpus list is derived from a
  corpus containing OCR-corrupted treatments, so a corruption
  recurring across 50+ documents could enter it.  A form here is
  verified Latin regardless.
* **Best combined coverage** — 76.8 % with both.

One cost to weigh: at 899 973 forms the vocabulary is large enough to
absorb some real corruption.  `taxon_43a7b19e` drops from 19.13 % to
16.22 % when this list is added, and to 18.67 % with both.  Still far
above any threshold in the 2–19 % band, but the direction is worth
knowing.  **The file is 11 MB**; if that is unwelcome in the
repository, dropping it costs 3.7 points of Latin coverage and
nothing on the detection cases.

## Reproducing

```sh
bin/whitakers_wordlist \
    --words-dir /data/piggy/src/github.com/mk270/whitakers-words \
    --output data/latin_wordlist.txt
```

The *WORDS* checkout is not vendored here.  Folding — lowercase,
macron stripping, `æ`/`œ` expansion — applies to the **word list
only**.  Never fold treatment text: rewriting it would invalidate
every stored `*_spans` offset.
