554 table rows: 390 Latin, 164 Greek.  Two kinds of form are
collected — the page's own Latin/Greek column (authoritative: these
are the forms used in systematic names) and our Latinization of the
Greek column in all three shapes a compound uses (bare
transliteration, Latin nominative, combining form).

## It validates the Latinizer

153 rows pair a Greek original with its accepted Latin form, which
makes this page a test set for
[`bin/latinize_greek.py`](../bin/latinize_greek.py):

| outcome | rows | share |
|---|---:|---:|
| exact match to a listed Latin form | 67 | 43.8 % |
| same stem, different ending | 80 | 52.3 % |
| **stem agreement** | **147** | **96.1 %** |
| no relation | 6 | 3.9 % |

All six non-matches are the page listing something other than a
transliteration, not letter-rule errors:

* βολβός → *bulbus* — the Latin **cognate**, whose ο→u is a
  borrowing irregularity rather than a rule;
* πούς → *pod-* — the **oblique stem**, where we give the
  nominative *pus*;
* γαῖα → *geo-* — a **different Greek lemma** (γῆ);
* ἔφαγον → *phago-* — the **root** φαγ-, not the aorist listed;
* ὑπέρ Βορέᾱ → *hyperboreus* — a **compound**, handled per word;
* -ούχος → *uchus* — leading-hyphen handling, since fixed.

The dominant ending disagreement, `-us` → `-o` (26 rows), is also
not an error: the page lists **combining forms** (*acantho-*), a
different lexical target from the nominative.  That prompted
`combining_form()`, so both shapes are now emitted.

849 forms, 432 new against English + WORDS, covering **7** of the 82
Latin forms in `taxon_d2a4c584` — against the DCC core list's **0**.
Latin coverage gains 1.3 points and `taxon_43a7b19e` ticks up rather
than down.  No poster child moves off 0.00 %.

So this is the right *kind* of vocabulary where the DCC core list
was the wrong kind — taxonomic roots rather than classical prose
frequency — but it is small, and it holds **roots** (*acantho-*,
*phyllo-*) rather than the inflected descriptive forms diagnoses
actually use (*albae*, *adscendentes*, *campanulatus*).  It is worth
keeping as a cheap, licence-clean supplement; it is not a substitute
for the corpus-derived vocabulary.
